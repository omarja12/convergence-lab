The θ-family on the log-transformed PDE

Solve the Black–Scholes PDE by stepping it forward in time with a tridiagonal system, in log-spot so every coefficient is constant. One parameter — θ — covers explicit, Crank–Nicolson, and implicit. The interesting part is not the family itself but the case where θ = ½ fails to deliver its advertised second order on a vanilla payoff, and the two half-steps that fix it.

The PDE, in log-spot

The Black–Scholes PDE for the option value V(S,t) is not solved in spot. It is solved in x = ln S, with time-to-maturity τ = T - t:

\[\frac{dV}{d\tau} = \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 \frac{d^2V}{dx^2} + (r - q - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2) \frac{dV}{dx} - rV\] \[(1 - \theta \cdot h \cdot L)\, V^{n+1} = (1 + (1-\theta) \cdot h \cdot L)\, V^n\] \[\frac{dV}{d\tau} = \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 \frac{d^2V}{dx^2} + (r - q - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2) \frac{dV}{dx} - rV\]

For a European contract the projection is skipped entirely. The step configuration — explicit, implicit, or Crank–Nicolson — is the same; only the American flag changes whether the projection runs.
